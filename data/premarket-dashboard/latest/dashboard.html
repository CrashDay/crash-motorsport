TSMC: Chips, Cash, and American Soil --------------------------------------- TSMC: Chips, Cash, and American Soil Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing just booked its sixth straight quarter of record profit. The world’s largest contract chipmaker builds the advanced processors most of the tech industry designs but doesn’t make. Third-quarter revenue topped management’s guidance while net income jumped 69% to a record NT$763.1 billion (about $23.9 billion). The broader sell-off in artificial intelligence (AI) stocks still sent the Team Hidden Gems recommendation down over 1% ahead of the open. * A margin beat during a ramp: As TSMC ramped up its new 2-nanometer process, gross margin dipped slightly. Operating margin of 58.7%, however, topped TSMC’s 58% guidance range. A manufacturer that keeps more than half of each sales dollar as operating profit holds incredible pricing power. AI customers have few other places to take their orders. High-performance computing, the chips behind AI and data centers, made up 68% of revenue. * Cash is funding the U.S. build-out: TSMC plans to invest $265 billion in Arizona and is reportedly weighing Texas as well. Management still expects Q4 operating margin of 56.5% to 58.5%. TSMC has the cash (its free cash flow jumped almost 66% in Q3), is racing to meet global demand, and is confident it will earn big margins throughout. Could TSM’s peer be worth $1 trillion? Rocket Lab Dives 10% as Neutron Slips to 2027 ------------------------------------------------ Rocket Lab ’s big rocket isn’t taking off just yet. On Wednesday, it said Neutron’s first flight has slipped to 2027. Neutron is a reusable medium-lift rocket that lets Rocket Lab bid for larger commercial, civil-space, and national-security contracts than its small Electron can carry. The Team Hidden Gems recommendation fell 10% in pre-market trading. * The slip was telegraphed in August: In August, Rocket Lab said it aimed to get Neutron to the launch pad in the fourth quarter. Rocket Lab plans to charge about $55 million per Neutron flight, compared with around $8.5 million per Electron flight. Each delay pushes that revenue further out. * Worth waiting for: Fool contributing analyst Lou Whiteman praised Rocket Lab’s transparency about its progress. He concluded, “Nothing is certain, but the long-range risk/reward hasn’t changed. If Neutron eventually flies, the quarter won’t matter, but until it does, it is the biggest single risk facing the stock.” The stock has outperformed the S&P 500 by 20% since the August 2025 Stock Advisor recommendation from Team Hidden Gems.
