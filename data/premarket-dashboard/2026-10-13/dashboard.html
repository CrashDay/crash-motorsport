David Gardner Keeps the 100-Bagger Dream Alive ------------------------------------------------- David Gardner Keeps the 100-Bagger Dream Alive At FoolFest 2026, a member put a question to David Gardner, The Motley Fool’s co-founder and Chief Rule Breaker: “How will we get 100-baggers when stocks that dominate IPOs come out at a valuation of $100 billion or more?” A 100-bagger turns $1,000 into $100,000. SpaceX listed in June at a valuation near $1.75 trillion. Multiply that by 100 and you need a $175 trillion company. David agreed the giants probably won’t get there. But he followed up: “in the next 30 years, there will be $50 trillion companies. That sounds crazy now, but so did a trillion about 20 years ago.” That puts a 100-bagger’s starting line near half a trillion dollars today. * David’s book, Rule Breaker Investing, shows where to look: Its first trait asks for the top dog and first mover in an important, emerging industry. The top two S&P 500 performers from 1995 through 2024, Nvidia and Amazon , both went public in that window. * Then you have to hold them: David’s formula is Habits × Traits. You multiply because a zero on either side wipes out the result. So the likeliest 100-baggers of the next 30 years are companies worth tens or hundreds of billions today, or less, that grow into the giants of 2056. David believes $50 trillion companies will sound as quaint then as a $438 million bookstore sounds today. Broadcom CEO’s “Casino” Remark Rattles Chips ----------------------------------------------- Broadcom , a Team Rule Breakers and Team Hidden Gems recommendation, makes custom AI chips for the biggest cloud companies. At a Monday event, CEO Hock Tan called the artificial intelligence (AI) trade a “casino,” saying that many investors “have no clue what they are investing in.” Ouch. That same day, China’s CXMT claimed progress in memory tech, and chip stocks sold off everywhere. In Asia, memory makers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell over 7% each. Micron , a Team Rule Breakers recommendation, closed around 4% lower on Monday and fell further in pre-market trading. * The warning comes from within the boom: Tan calls the market’s expectations unrealistic. That’s a notable admission from someone who benefits from the boom. Broadcom also supplies custom chips to OpenAI, and Tan said OpenAI’s current sales pace says little about long-term profitability. * Fundamentals remain strong for now: Chip earnings today depend on AI customers continuing to spend. That spending, however, depends on external funding and unproven returns. The risk sits in the chain between suppliers and capital markets. A long-term investor should track what improves that chain—customers raising more money and products earning back—and what breaks it.
